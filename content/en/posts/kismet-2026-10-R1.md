## 2026-10-R1

Insert the usual mea culpa here that it's been way too long since the last
release.  It has been.  But at least this is a good one, and sets the framework
for a lot of future development, and builds a lot of tooling to make future
updates simpler.

This release brings a ton of performance fixes, new phy types, new data sources,
and bug fixes.

## Performance

- **Web UI and REST API:** fetching a full device list as JSON is roughly 300 times
  faster on large lists.
- **Packet processing:** the packet chain uses lock-free handler snapshots,
  per-thread de-duplication and batched reads, and keeps an AP's packets on one
  thread.
- **Lookups:** the manufacturer, Bluetooth and ICAO databases are cached and no longer
  looked up under the device list lock.
- **Memory:** about 14% less per device from the packet and pool changes, a further
  2.8 KB per device from storing RRDs inline, and about 0.65 MB per 5000 APs from not
  keeping empty 802.11d countries.

## New datasources

- **Cellular modems** (`cellat`, `celldiag`): serving and neighbouring cell towers from
  Quectel LTE / 5G modems, over AT commands (`cellat`, with optional band locking) or
  Qualcomm DIAG (`celldiag`). Towers are tracked as devices, with a Cell Info panel
  in the web UI. The DIAG source raises EFF Rayhunter-style alerts for suspicious
  network behaviour: null ciphering or integrity (`CELLNULLCIPHER`,
  `CELLNULLINTEGRITY`), 2G redirects (`CELL2GREDIRECT`), incomplete system information
  (`CELLINCOMPLETESIB`) and identity requests (`CELLIDREQUEST`, `CELLIDREQNOAUTH`).
  The modem's GNSS can act as a Kismet GPS, and detailed cell logs are kept in the
  kismetdb and extracted with the new `kismetdb_to_celllog`. Modems with no host
  driver, such as on macOS, are reached over libusb, and the new `kismet_usbserial`
  tool serves their AT, DIAG, NMEA and modem ports as pseudo-terminals
  (`--disable-usbserial` turns it off). The sources are hardened for suid installs.
  (Luke Jenkins)
- **Userspace USB Wi-Fi** (`usbwifi`): monitor mode capture on MediaTek MT7921U,
  MT7925U and MT7612U / MT7632U, and Realtek RTL8812AU / 8811AU, RTL8821AU, RTL8814AU,
  RTL8812BU / 8822BU, RTL8812CU / 8822CU, RTL8811CU / 8821CU and RTL8723DU adapters.
  It runs the Linux mt76 and rtw88 drivers in userspace on libusb, so it also works on
  macOS, with no VM. Firmware is included, and on Linux it's also loaded from the
  system firmware directories, zstd or xz compressed. Of the Realtek chips only the
  RTL8814AU has been tested, and Realtek adapters stay in USB 2 mode. On Wi-Fi +
  Bluetooth combo adapters, Bluetooth keeps working on Linux.
- **Morse Micro MM8108 USB HaLow** (`mm8108`): 802.11ah capture with a userspace
  driver on Linux and macOS, with firmware and board configurations included. It hops
  no faster than every 2 seconds to catch slow HaLow beacons, and several adapters
  can capture at once.
- **RTL-SDR AIS** (`rtlais`) and **AIS + ADS-B** (`rtlaisadsb`): ships, base
  stations, aids to navigation, SAR aircraft and AIS-SART / MOB / EPIRB beacons, on
  both AIS channels at once, with an AIS map and NMEA AIVDM websocket feeds for
  OpenCPN. `rtlaisadsb` hops one radio between AIS and ADS-B.
- **802.15.4 and Zigbee:** CatSniffer V3 (`catsniffer_zigbee`, shrout1, RFDeck), and
  legacy 900 MHz 802.15.4 on an RTL-SDR (`zigbee900sdr`, shrout1).
- **BLE:** Sniffle on a Sonoff ZBDongle-P (`sniffle_ble`, shrout1), and the WCH BLE
  Analyzer Pro (`wch-ble-pro`, yi chen).
- **LoRa:** radios running LoraPipe firmware (`lorapipe`), with Meshtastic regional
  channel presets.

## New features

- **Reported locations kept separate (major change):** devices that report their own
  position (ADS-B, AIS, Remote ID, MeshCore, Meshtastic) keep it in
  `kismet.device.base.reported_location`, and the device location always means where
  Kismet was when it heard them. kismetdb (v11), the maps and `kismetdb_to_kml` /
  `kismetdb_to_gpx` follow suit. See Upgrading.
- **Bluetooth and BTLE decoding:**
  - Advertising and EIR data are decoded: company and service names, Device ID,
    Apple Continuity, Microsoft, Fast Pair, Matter, mesh, LE Audio, sensors, iBeacon,
    AltBeacon and Eddystone IDs, class of device, appearance, advertised battery
    levels, and an estimated distance from the advertised transmit power.
  - **Location trackers:** Apple Find My (AirTag and network accessories), Google
    FMDN, DULT, Samsung SmartTag, Tile, Chipolo and Pebblebee tags are recognized,
    with state, battery and whether they're separated from their owner (RFDeck),
    and get their own icon in the web UI. A new `TRACKERFOLLOW` alert is raised for a
    separated tracker that moves with you.
  - Random addresses are labelled static, resolvable or non-resolvable, and Wi-Fi and
    BTLE devices that rotate their address can optionally be linked
    (`dot11_link_randomized`, `btle_link_randomized`, off by default).
  - HCI captures (H4, Linux monitor and HCI pcaps) create devices with the remote
    version, chip maker and firmware.
  - Ubertooth can survey classic Bluetooth piconets (`mode=classic`), and Linux
    Bluetooth can actively probe for names or version and features (`probe=name|info`,
    off by default).
  - Linux HCI reports RSSI, connectability, service UUIDs and manufacturer data
    (cadneowl, Matt Miller, shrout1), and resumes scanning after a controller is reset.
  - nRF51822 defaults to the Nordic firmware's 460800 baud (Sebastian Reitenbach), and
    it and Sniffle filter packets that fail CRC (shrout1).
- **802.15.4, Zigbee and Thread** (Textile, RFDeck):
  - IEEE 802.15.4-2015 frames, the TAP header read by its TLVs, FCS checking, and the
    SUN PHYs' long frames.
  - Zigbee, Thread and 6LoWPAN recognized above the MAC, with device roles, network
    names, extended PAN IDs and EUI-64 learned from the NWK header.
  - **Zigbee key checks:** frames are checked against the publicly known keys
    (ZigBeeAlliance09, ZLL, Z-Stack, Nordic, Legrand and others) and keys sent in the
    clear, raising a `ZIGBEEWEAKKEY` alert. Your own keys can be added with
    `zigbee_key=` in the new `kismet_zigbee.conf`; key values are shown only with
    `zigbee_show_keys`.
  - Short addresses are tracked per PAN, and destination addresses no longer create
    devices.
- **Meshtastic and LoRa:** Meshtastic decoding, including encrypted channels with the
  default or configured keys (`kismet_meshtastic.conf`), with node, telemetry and
  message tracking; the LoRaTAP format.
- **ANT / ANT+ and nRF24:** heart-rate, bike, foot pod and fitness devices, and
  Logitech / Microsoft nRF24 keyboards and mice, from the nRF24 research dongle or an
  SDR capture file, with the `NRFPLAINTEXTKEYS` alert for unencrypted keystrokes.
  (Textile)
- **802.11:**
  - channel switch announcements, with the `DOT11CSACH14`, `DOT11CSAFLOOD` and
    `DOT11CSASPOOF` alerts (RFDeck);
  - Broadcom SDK WPS daemon detection, with the `BCMWPSMONITOR`, `WPSFRAGLEN` and
    `WPSFRAGPROBE` alerts;
  - clients with randomized (locally administered) MACs are flagged;
  - initial 802.11ah / HaLow (S1G) decoding.
- **ADS-B:** aircraft from the RTL-SDR sources get a signal level, carried in the Beast
  feed, and new aircraft get their manufacturer.
- **Remote capture:** tools try every address of the server's name, so IPv6 and `--tcp`
  over IPv6 work; connecting `--tcp` to the web server port says so; and probe and
  websocket errors are readable.
- **GPS:** serial GPS autodetection (baud rate, NMEA / u-blox UBX / SiRF), signal
  quality (satellites, DOP, estimated error), and the satellites in view, shown in the
  web UI and logged to kismetdb as `GPSSATS` every `kis_log_gps_satellites_rate`
  seconds.
- **Logging:** the in-memory `pcapng_ring` log with on-demand snapshots (Costa),
  WiGLE CSV 1.6 with frequency and Bluetooth devices, signal levels on kismetdb data
  records (v10), and JSON packet data logged directly.
- **REST API:** new endpoints for AIS map data and AIVDM feeds, deleting a device tag,
  devices modified since a time (RFDeck), pcapng ring snapshots and Meshtastic
  channels; many new tracked fields (GPS, Bluetooth, 802.15.4, AIS, Meshtastic,
  datasource packet counts, and per-device min / max signal history); and
  `kismet.datasource.channel_spans`, the frequencies each SDR source's channels cover.
- **Console status display:** started on a terminal, Kismet shows the web UI address,
  per-source packet graphs, device and memory graphs, and server messages.
- **Web UI:** an 802.15.4 device panel (Textile), Cell Info, broader censor mode
  (`censor_heavy=1`), invalid / filtered / dropped counts per source, and a hop-rate
  control that respects the driver's maximum.
- **Configuration:** `kismet_site.d/` override directory, per-source
  `channel_hoprate=`, and `datasource_list_timeout` so a slow capture tool can't hang
  the interface list.

## Bug fixes

### Security

- **Capture and remote capture:** frame lengths could wrap past the size checks on
  32-bit systems, a short legacy v2 frame could be over-read, and BladeRF trusted the
  radio's packet length (Tristan). The remote capture protocol was hardened against
  oversized lengths, unterminated strings, an undersized peek and a use-after-free,
  and capture agents not on localhost can no longer fetch an HTTP auth token
  (`remote_capture_allow_http_auth` restores it). (Daniel Birtwhistle)
- **Web server:** a `..` path could read files from a directory next to the web root
  whose name starts with the web root's name (CWE-22, sighook); API keys with an
  expiration never expired (RFDeck); and slow or stalled clients are disconnected
  after 30 seconds instead of holding a thread.
- **`hidedata=true`** now cuts data frames to their headers in the logs and streams,
  not just in decoding. (RFDeck)
- **Malformed packets** no longer read out of bounds or crash the server: PPI GPS
  (sighook), radiotap, Mode S, CC2540, 802.15.4 tap headers and 802.11 management
  frames and EAP identities (Ibrahim AlJaafreh), nRF52840 records, radiation spectra,
  rtl_433 meter records, and REST API field paths that treat a value as a map.
- **Leaked memory contents:** WEP-decrypted frames carried uninitialized memory in
  place of their 802.11 header, and nRF51822 packets carried unset stack memory.
- **Other hardening:** JSON output escaping, NaN / Inf and number range;
  `munge_to_printable()` output size; RRD samples with negative timestamps; the Linux
  BTLE sysfs path (Daniel Birtwhistle); capture helper line buffers; stack buffers
  sized from packet data in several datasources; and an OpenBSD websocket
  use-after-free (Sebastian Reitenbach).

### Server and datasources

- **Capture framework:** several helpers exiting at once are all reaped, queued output
  is no longer thrown away when a write would block, frames go out without up to
  500 ms delay, and reporting config before a channel is set no longer crashes.
- **Datasources:** an open failure says why, over HTTP too, including when a driver
  declined the source; a stale error clears once the source opens; a graceful close
  that times out no longer hangs; `block_channels=`, duplicate channels and hop skip
  calculations are fixed; closing an unknown UUID no longer closes the last source
  (RFDeck); a missing capture tool no longer aborts Kismet; and plugin datasources
  open at startup (RFDeck).
- **Packet handling:** crashes removing a handler under load (RFDeck), deadlocks
  under heavy load and web use, purged devices staying in views, and timer
  cancellation are fixed. Unchanging frames repeated more than a second apart (BTLE
  adverts, ADS-B, AIS, sensors) now count as new packets instead of duplicates.
- **GPS:** gpsd is retried when it isn't running at startup; GPS data is no longer
  lost once packets are recycled, peak locations aren't set to 0,0, per-source `gps=`
  doesn't override v3 sources (weebl); a source's own GPS that loses its fix no
  longer stamps packets with its last position; `device_location_signal_threshold_dbm`
  works again.
- **Web server:** logins set in the web UI are read back after a restart; clients
  holding a non-admin token are no longer refused on routes that need no login
  (RFDeck); a port already in use is reported.
- **Logging and replay:** kismetdb replay keeps each packet's frequency and survives
  damaged logs; the kismetdb close race (#644, RFDeck); `kismetdb_to_pcap` crashes
  (Sebastian Reitenbach); pcap and kismetdb realtime replay timing; and the pcap
  end-of-file message being lost.
- **Channel tracker:** per-second packet and byte counts were averaged, and device
  counts dropped on every read.

### Protocols and capture tools

- **802.11:** ad-hoc misdetection and the `WMMTSPEC` alert (RFDeck), `last_bssid`
  overwritten by probes (RFDeck), VHT operation parsing (weebl), mesh SSID checksums,
  WPS details and the `WPSBRUTE` alert, frames captured short failing the FCS check
  (RFDeck), and an empty 802.11d country kept for every AP.
- **Bluetooth:** BLE CRC and channel handling, packets with a CRC the radio already
  validated, the `FLIPPERZERO` alert for BTLE (RFDeck), and devices that only send a
  shortened name now get it.
- **802.15.4:** per-packet parse state across threads, FCS failures counted as
  invalid, and BTLE data channel packets no longer creating devices. (Textile, RFDeck)
- **Capture tools:** Linux Wi-Fi (renamed wiphys, RFDeck; interface listing, HT40
  bands, wext fallback, netlink hangs), OpenBSD Wi-Fi channels (Sebastian Reitenbach),
  macOS CoreWLAN channel lists, rtl_433 (now always `-s 1024k`) and rtladsb,
  nRF52840 (full-size frames, and the macOS hardware flow control flag), RZ Raven /
  KillerBee, Ubertooth, mousejack, TI CC2531 / CC2540, KW41Z, FreakLabs, Radiacode
  firmware 5.x, AntSDR DroneID, Coconut, BladeRF, MQTT TLS keys, the Python rtl_433
  and BT Geiger helpers, and serial sources spinning on an unplugged device.
- **Web UI:** default units follow the config on a clean install (Wessel); the device
  view selector no longer leaks menu elements (nikqig) or resets while open; the device
  list keeps its scroll position and row heights (Textile); and popups, the hop rate
  menu and the datasource panel's controls behave across refreshes and theme changes.
- **Misc:** `is_multicast()` / `is_broadcast()` (RFDeck), base64 encoding, the plugin
  tracker (#602), saved devices with NULL names or tags, and scan report values.

## Misc

- **Databases:** the manufacturer database merges IEEE, Wireshark and Nmap, about
  86,000 entries (Ringmast4r), and the Bluetooth company and ID databases are
  installed and refreshed for each release.
- **Removed:** the deprecated Python rtlamr source and Python rtladsb helper (the C
  rtladsb helper replaces it), and `kismet.channeltracker.channel`.
- **Build and platform:** OpenBSD fixes (Sebastian Reitenbach); macOS / BSD fixes;
  libfmt 12.1, nlohmann::json 3.12.0 and backward-cpp updates (Steev Klimaszewski);
  optional zstd / xz firmware support; C++17 `std::string_view`; fewer compiler
  warnings; Docker-based CI; a release script that checks the tree and test builds
  before tagging; and documentation fixes (Tim Smith).

### Upgrading

- **Device locations:** for ADS-B, AIS, Remote ID, MeshCore and Meshtastic devices,
  `kismet.device.base.location`, `location_cloud` and the kismetdb location columns
  now hold Kismet's GPS position. Read `reported_location` / `reported_location_cloud`
  or the kismetdb `reported_*` columns for the device's own position.
- **kismetdb** logs are now version 11; older tools may not read the new data.
- **API keys** with an expiration now expire, and remote capture agents not on
  localhost need `remote_capture_allow_http_auth=true` to fetch an auth token.
- **Alerts:** `BLEEDINGTOOTH` and `DISCONCODEINVALID` are off by default.
- **Device keys:** 802.15.4 short-address devices are keyed by PAN and short address,
  and nRF24 dongle devices by their 5-byte address, so both show up as new devices.
  Devices wrongly marked as Wi-Fi ad-hoc keep that type in a saved device database
  until it's cleared.
- **Removed sources:** remove `rtlamr` sources and MQTT `mapping=RTLamr` from configs;
  the `python3-kismetcapturertladsb` package can be removed.
- **Channel tracker** clients using `kismet.channeltracker.channel` need updating.
- **Strings** with NUL or non-UTF-8 bytes now show escaped (`\u0000`, `\xNN`).
